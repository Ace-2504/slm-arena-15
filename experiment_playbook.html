Tier 1 · The must-dos (→ ~93–95%)

1 Check the AI judge against a human — you

The single most important experiment. Without it, one reviewer sentence can sink the paper.

Why it matters Right now every number depends on one AI judge nobody checked. A reviewer will say "how do you know the judge is right?" This answers it with data.
What to do You
  1. Pick ~10 answers from each of the 13 models (≈130 total), spread across case-law / SEC / web. This "spread" is called a stratified sample — it just means don't grade only the good models.
  2. For each one, read the question + the gold answer + the model's answer, and give your own 0–10 using the same rubric (correctness 0–5, completeness 0–2, groundedness 0–2, clarity 0–1). Also tick fabricated? yes/no.
  3. Do it blind — hide the AI judge's score while you grade, so you're not influenced.
  4. Put your score next to the AI's score in the file and send it to me.
What I compute & where it goes Claude Agreement between you and the judge: weighted Cohen's κ, correlation, and "% within 1 point." Good result: κ ≥ 0.6, correlation ≥ 0.7. Goes into a new section "Judge calibration."
Watch out: grade blind, and include the near-zero models too — if you only grade Gemma, the agreement looks fake. This is the long pole: start it today.
Your time: 6–8 hCost: ₹0Kills: "single unchecked judge"

2 Get a second, different AI judge to re-grade

If two different AI judges rank your models the same way, no one can call it a judge quirk.

Why it matters One judge has its own blind spots. A second judge from a different family (you use Gemini → add a GPT-class or Claude judge) proves the ranking isn't an artifact of that one model.
What to do You Claude
  1. Re-score the same answers (all 500, or a 150 subset if budget is tight) with a different judge model, using the exact same rubric and prompt.
  2. I'll hand you the scoring script; you just plug in the other API key and run it. Keep everything else identical.
  3. Send me both judges' scores.
What I compute Claude Rank correlation between the two judges (Spearman / Kendall) and whether the leaderboard order stays the same. Add one line: "the ranking is stable across two independent judges."
Watch out: same prompt, same rubric, same questions — only the judge model changes. Any other difference muddies the comparison.
Your time: 2–4 h + a short API runCost: a few $Kills: "single-judge bias"

3 Put error bars on the leaderboard

Turns "9.80 beats 9.74" into a defensible claim (or an honest "these two tie").

Why it matters A leaderboard with no error bars invites "is 3.60 really different from 3.69?" Confidence intervals answer that up front.
What I do (you do nothing but export) Claude
  1. Bootstrap confidence intervals — I re-sample your 500 questions thousands of times to get a 95% range for each model's score. (Plain meaning: "if we'd asked a slightly different 500 questions, the score would land in this band.")
  2. Paired significance tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank) between neighbouring models, with a correction for testing many pairs — so we only claim a gap is real when it is.
Where it goesError bars added to the results table + a new short section "Statistical significance."
Watch out: nothing on your side — this is pure re-analysis of the file from Step 0. No new model runs, no cost.
Your time: ~0Cost: ₹0Kills: "no error bars / no significance"

4 Prove the fabrication finding is real

Your best, most original result — make it statistically bulletproof.

Why it matters "Alignment increases fabrication" (Gemma 0.2% → 29.4%) is the paper's headline. Right now it's a number in a table; a test makes it a proven result.
What I do Claude A McNemar test — the right test when the same questions are answered by "before alignment" and "after alignment," and you're comparing a yes/no flag (fabricated or not). I report the p-value and effect size for base-vs-RLAIF and base-vs-DPO.
What to do YouNothing extra — it uses the fabricated flags already in your Step-0 file. (Bonus: hand-confirm ~40 of the flagged answers really do fabricate, folded into Experiment 1.)
Watch out: make sure the fabrication flag is per-question and paired across stages, or the test can't run.
Your time: ~0Cost: ₹0Kills: "is the fabrication jump just noise?"

Tier 2 · The boosters (→ ~97–98%)

5 Re-run the alignment with 2–3 random seeds

Shows your alignment results (and the fabrication jump) aren't one lucky/unlucky run.

Why it matters Training has randomness. Doing it once could be a fluke. Running the same recipe 2–3 times with different seeds (the random starting number) and reporting the average ± spread proves it's a real effect.
What to do You
  1. Take your DPO and RLAIF recipes (the stages that matter most) and re-run each 2 more times with a different seed, for the key model sizes.
  2. Re-score each with the judge; report mean ± standard deviation across seeds.
  3. These mostly run unattended on Modal — launch them and walk away.
Watch out: this is the only experiment that needs GPU time and can slip if your pipeline is flaky. It's a booster — if the clock gets tight, skip it; Tier 1 already clears the bar. Launch it early so it finishes in the background.
Your time: 2–3 h to launchWall-clock: 3–6 days (unattended)Cost: ~$10–30

6 Release the code + eval set (anonymised)

"You can run this yourself" is nearly free acceptance points.

Why it mattersReviewers trust and reward work they can reproduce. An open repo signals confidence and rigor.
What to do You Claude
  1. Put the 500-question eval set, the judge prompt/rubric, the scoring code, and at least a couple of checkpoints in a repo.
  2. Make it anonymous for review (no name, no personal GitHub) — use an anonymised mirror; reveal the real link only in the final camera-ready.
  3. I'll write the README and the dataset card for you.
Watch out: double-blind means the repo, the arena URL, and your name must NOT appear in the submitted PDF. Reveal them only after acceptance.
Your time: 3–5 hCost: ₹0Kills: "is this reproducible?"

7 Check the judge for the well-known AI-judge biases

Shows the informed reviewer you already thought of their objection.

Why it mattersAI judges are known to sometimes favour longer answers, answer order, or their own family's style. Showing you checked these makes the judge credible.
What I do Claude From your Step-0 file: correlate answer length vs score (verbosity bias), note you already score one answer at a time (kills order bias), and check the Gemini judge doesn't over-reward Gemma/AI-aligned outputs (self-preference). One tight paragraph in the methods.
Watch out: if a bias does show up, that's fine — report it honestly as a limitation. Honesty reads as strength.
Your time: ~1 hCost: ₹0Kills: "did you control for judge bias?"
